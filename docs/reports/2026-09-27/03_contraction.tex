\section{Contraction and the continuous calculation}\label{sec:contraction}
The contraction calculation starts from a fully convective model at
$\Teff=2864$ K and $R=1.594\Rs$. Initial deuterium burns first and is
exhausted; proton--proton burning then rises to supply the surface
luminosity (Figure~\ref{fig:pms}). Near 1 Gyr the model has
$\Teff=\pmsTemperature$ K, $R=\pmsRadius\Rs$,
$L=\pmsLuminosity\Ls$, central temperature \pmsCoreTemperature\ MK and
$\log g=5.234$, with nuclear burning supplying \pmsNuclearPercent\% of the light.
The abundances are uniform, $X=0.6998$ and $X_3=\pmsHeThree$.
The first \pmsAccepted\ accepted intervals reach this state without rejections;
each interval passes the time-error, isotope, mass-defect and first-law checks.

During deuterium burning the convective travel time is 0.9535--1.415 yr,
compared with local burning times of about 2000 yr or longer. The omitted
heat carried by composition redistribution remains below 0.01\% of the
local luminosity. After deuterium exhaustion that heat is included.
Instantaneous mixing is checked against the abundance gradients required
to carry the reconstructed material flux within each convective region.
Microscopic species exchange is followed explicitly across hot radiative
boundaries when they form; cooler radiative exchange needs additional
transport physics.

\paragraph{Comparison with observed stars.}
Table~\ref{tab:observed} compares the continuous model near 1 Gyr with
three measured objects \cite{davis24,agol21,ribas17}. The closest comparison
is the eclipsing companion EBLM J2114$-$39 B, whose mass agrees with
$0.1\Ms$ and whose radius is about 1.2\% smaller than Ember's, within its
uncertainty. Its measured metallicity is $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]=-0.001\pm0.032$;
a precise comparison would also match the initial helium and metal abundances. TRAPPIST-1 and Proxima bracket Ember in radius,
temperature and luminosity, but their masses rest on a mass--luminosity
relation and on stellar models respectively. Ten billion years of hydrogen
burning changes Ember's luminosity by less than 0.4\% and its temperature
by less than 2 K, so main-sequence age does not affect the comparison.
The agreement supports the starting structure without establishing
percent-level accuracy or selecting among atmosphere and interior choices.

\paragraph{Continuous hydrogen burning.}
With composition-dependent atmospheres and hot microscopic transport, the
Hayashi-started calculation reaches $\continuousAge\Tyr$ in about
\continuousAccepted\ accepted intervals and \continuousRejected\ rejected
trials. At that point surface $X=\continuousHydrogen$, $\Teff=\continuousTemperature$ K,
$R=\continuousRadius\Rs$, $L=\continuousLuminosity\Ls$, and the central
temperature is \continuousCoreTemperature\ MK. Nuclear burning supplies
\continuousNuclearPercent\% of the surface luminosity. A radiative shell
first appears at $3.558\Tyr$, separating the convective centre from the
envelope. The centre subsequently becomes radiative; the radiative interior
contains a helium-rich centre and a hydrogen-burning shell beneath the
convective envelope. Convection occupies 1.800\% of the total mass. Microscopic diffusion is active across the radiative
interfaces, while convection mixes each connected convective region.
Central hydrogen is $X=3.472\times10^{-8}$. Helium-3 is depleted to
$X_3=8.893\times10^{-15}$ at the centre but reaches $X_3=0.01862$ at
$m/M=0.9715$. Nuclear heating peaks at $m/M=0.9669$, with
$\epsilon_{\rm nuc}=0.5040$ erg g$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$. Surface temperature
passed its maximum of 4786 K at $3.841\Tyr$ and has declined since.
The remaining helium-3 mass is $7.776\times10^{28}$ g; its presence alone
does not establish that a flash will occur.

A fixed-metal atmosphere comparison reaches $3.078\Tyr$, 3130 K and
$X=0.2629$, also without rejected intervals. The composition-dependent
selection spans the saved settling structures, including their trace-helium
and nearly pure-hydrogen surfaces. All sources join at optical depth 100;
the hydrogen-dominated family reaches 4550 K through $\log g=6.2$, with
intermediate knots interpolated from solved columns. Trace helium remains
approximated, and colder atmosphere coverage remains incomplete.
The continuous model has passed the age of the comparison model's flash,
without a runaway. The clocks and boundary histories differ, so this is
not a matched test of ignition or evidence that later flashes are excluded.
Tracks S, F and P supply the late hydrogen-burning comparisons in
Section~\ref{sec:hydrogen}.

A 50 Gyr time-resolution comparison in the fully convective phase supports
an absolute species time-error tolerance of $10^{-7}$: it uses 25 intervals
instead of 73 at $10^{-8}$, with maximum relative density difference
$7.451\times10^{-6}$ and helium-3 inventory difference $1.502\times10^{-5}$.
In the radiative-core phase, a matched 500 Myr comparison supports
$10^{-5}$: the largest temperature difference is $2.475\times10^{-6}$
relative and the total helium-3 difference is $2.766\times10^{-5}$ relative,
compared with $10^{-6}$. It uses 17 accepted intervals instead of 45.
The structure tolerance remains $10^{-4}$ and the nuclear-power tolerance
0.005. A separate 1 Gyr comparison supports a local Newton correction
criterion of $10^{-12}$, with outer composition coupling at $10^{-13}$ and
integrated species balance at $10^{-14}$. The maximum temperature difference
is $2.615\times10^{-7}$ relative. All global conservation checks remain
unchanged. These comparisons do not establish convergence during a flash.
